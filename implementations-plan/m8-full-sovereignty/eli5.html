Milestone 8 · Full Sovereignty

Your Ledger is your Aztec account

Phase 7 & 8, explained for a smart friend who isn't a cryptographer.

The one-sentence goal

Make it so that everything about your private Aztec account comes from your hardware wallet — not just the key that spends your money, but also the keys that let you see your money — and prove it with a worst-case test: wipe the computer clean, plug the Ledger back in, and watch the exact same account reappear.

Why this matters. A hardware wallet (a Ledger) is a tiny sealed device that holds your secret keys so that even a hacked computer can't steal them. On most blockchains the Ledger only does one job: sign a transaction. But Aztec is private — to even read your own balance you need extra "viewing keys." The big question of M8: can the Ledger be the trustworthy home for those too? If yes, the device alone is your whole wallet, and you never have to trust the computer.

What the device can already do (Phases 0–6, built & tested)

We taught the Ledger to grow its own viewing keys from its secret seed, entirely on-chip, and to refuse to create an account unless the computer's numbers exactly match the device's own. A scammer can no longer slip in their own viewing keys during setup — the device checks the math itself and rejects mismatches. proven on a real emulated device

So the hard cryptography is done and verified. What's left is the human half: actually using those device keys in the browser, and proving you can always get back in. That's Phase 7 and 8.

Phase 7 — Onboarding (being handed the keys)

Connecting is plugging in the device and being shown which key is which. There's nothing extra to file away afterwards — the device already remembers everything.

"Derive my viewing keys"

The first time you connect, you press a clearly-labelled button. The Ledger hands the viewing keys to the browser (so it can show your private balance) while the spending key stays sealed inside forever. One deliberate tap, clearly worded — never silent, so a scam site can't trick you into it.

The surprise we found. Today the browser was quietly inventing random keys instead of asking the Ledger. With the new Phase-6 safety checks, that random key gets rejected by the device every single time. So onboarding isn't decoration — it's the missing wire that makes the whole milestone actually work. It was the headline finding of the code review, and it's exactly what Phase 7 fixes.

The backup? There isn't a separate one — and that's the point

Every Ledger is already backed up the day you set it up: you write down its 24-word recovery phrase. Your Aztec viewing keys are grown from that same seed in a fixed, repeatable way. So the device (or any device restored from its phrase) can always re-grow the identical keys.

So we deliberately removed the extra backup. An earlier draft made a separate 24-word Aztec phrase plus a little "note card" file. The owner pointed out that's redundant with the Ledger's own backup — and keeping the viewing root on paper is weaker than keeping it only on the device. Less to build, less to lose, better security.

Phase 8 — The clean-machine test (the hero moment)

Wipe everything off the computer. Reload. Plug the Ledger back in, tap "derive" once… and your account and balance are simply there again.

  1. Wipe all the browser's saved data — as blank as a brand-new machine.
  2. Reconnect the Ledger and tap "derive viewing keys" once.
  3. The device re-grows the exact same keys; the app recomputes the exact same account address.
  4. Your balance reappears — and because the Ledger is plugged in, you can spend again too, not just look.
Why this is stronger than "type your words on a new laptop." The secret never sits on a disk or a piece of paper waiting to be stolen — it's re-grown fresh from the sealed device each session and forgotten when you close the tab. The device is the single source of truth. Lost the physical Ledger? Restore it from its own recovery phrase onto a new one — same seed, same Aztec account. The Aztec layer needs nothing extra.

The handful of decisions worth understanding

The "salt" that makes it all work

An account's address is mixed from your keys plus a tiny pinch called "salt." It used to be random, so reconnecting would have produced a different address and shown an empty account. The fix — using a fixed, predictable salt — is the small change that makes "plug back in → same account" true every time. It's now the most load-bearing piece of the whole plan. The plan review caught this before we wrote any code.

We dropped the device-less viewing path (for now)

We considered letting someone see a balance on a machine with no Ledger at all (handy for, say, showing an auditor). It's a genuinely cool Aztec capability — but it means putting the viewing key somewhere off the device, which fights the "device is the only home" idea. The ability still exists under the hood; we just won't ship a button for it. Simpler, and truer to the security story.

Two device tweaks we chose not to do

  • No extra locks on the "derive" screen — just clearer wording ("this lets this computer see your private balance"). Extra locks mean re-opening already-tested device code for little real gain in a demo.
  • No re-engineering of the key recipe — a "cleaner" version exists, but it would force re-testing all the device cryptography, and its only benefit appears if the device itself is fully hacked — at which point you've already lost everything.

How we pressure-tested the plan

We asked a second, different AI (Codex) to try to break things — twice: once on the code already written, once on the plan. It found real holes (the salt mistake; a wrong assumption about how recovery finds your notes). Then the owner — who knows the Aztec internals cold — corrected three more assumptions and, in doing so, let us delete about 60% of the remaining work while making the story better. Good planning looks like throwing away the parts you don't need.

Genuinely solved, no longer worried about

  • How a fresh wallet finds your old notes: the Aztec library handles it automatically once the wallet is set up correctly — no custom machinery needed.
  • Speed: finding the notes is fast; no special timing tricks required.
  • What we still verify carefully: that the re-grown address matches the original exactly, byte for byte. If it ever didn't, that's a loud error — never quietly the wrong account.
